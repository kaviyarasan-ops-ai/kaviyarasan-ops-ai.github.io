How I think about operations

Principles, not tactics. Each one comes from something I've run and written about.

01
Fewer reasons to contact beats faster support
A seller ticket is usually the end of a problem that began upstream days earlier. The biggest wins come from fixing that step.
02
Ticket data is process data
Support teams see the symptoms first. That makes them the best early-warning system a business has, as long as leadership treats their data that way.
03
Know exactly where AI should not act
Before automating anything, I ask what happens when it's confidently wrong. AI takes volume and speed. People keep judgment wherever money, account standing or trust is at stake.
04
Watch the tail, not the average
The average tells you how the system usually behaves. The worst 10% tells you who it lets down, and that shapes how sellers talk about you.
05
Standardise 90%, let 10% vary
One core process, one set of definitions, and a short list of local exceptions per market, reviewed every quarter.
06
Build problem-solvers, not dependencies
My job isn't to have every answer. It's to build a team where answers come faster than problems. A promotion is a bet on a new job, so I back it with a 90-day plan.

The leaders who get this right won't be the ones who automate the most. They'll be the ones who know exactly where not to.From my LinkedIn post on AI in support
